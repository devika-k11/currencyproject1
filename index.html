Strengthening Vidyut Raksha: Expert Feedback from Dr. Premanand B

How a faculty consultation improved our electrified-floodwater warning system

Posted by Team CYGNUS | 30 September 2026

Dr. Premanand B

Head of Department, Vidya Academy of Science and Technology (VAST), Thrissur

A retired faculty member of Government Engineering College (GEC), Dr. Premanand now leads his department at VAST and teaches Control Systems. He was consulted for his expertise in control systems and electrical engineering principles relevant to the project's sensing and safety-threshold design.

The Consultation

Project Presented

Vidyut Raksha is a compact, submersible sensor node designed to detect electrified floodwater in real time and warn nearby pedestrians before they enter a hazardous area. It uses voltage-gradient sensing between probes, based on step-potential principles from electrical safety engineering, and issues a two-tier alert: an immediate local warning via offline Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, and a wider, authority-linked warning through India's SACHET Cell Broadcast system. The team presented the sensing mechanism, the 12V AC safety threshold, and the overall system architecture.

Discussion Summary

Dr. Premanand acknowledged the idea as good and relevant, addressing a real, under-served safety gap. He then raised three substantive technical concerns.

Feedback 1: Locating the extent of the hazard

He questioned how effectively a voltage-gradient measurement from a single probe pair could estimate the exact extent of electrification in the water, that is, how far the hazard spreads from its source.

Feedback 2: Current path through water vs. earth

He pointed out that if a live wire falls on a road, the road and the earth beneath already act as a ground path. Since soil conducts electricity, he questioned whether standing water would carry enough current to be hazardous independently.

Feedback 3: LoRa communication and tower dependence

He also asked whether LoRa communication is tower-based, like mobile networks, and what the system needs in order to communicate.

Team's Response and Improvements

LoRa communication

The team clarified that LoRa is not tower-based. Unlike a mobile network, it does not depend on cellular tower infrastructure. Research into this point showed that the node needs only two things for communication: the specified LoRa module and an antenna.

Localization

The team acknowledged that a single probe pair measures the gradient only at its own location. The sensing design was upgraded to a four-probe quadrupole cross array (two orthogonal pairs), allowing the node to calculate both the magnitude and bearing of the voltage gradient using vector resolution. Combined with the node's fixed GPS coordinate and a distance estimate from step-potential decay, the system projects a probable hazard coordinate. It is presented as an estimated bearing and distance, not an exact measurement, since real floodwater is not a uniform conductive medium.

Current path

Current splits between all parallel paths, earth and surface water, in proportion to their conductivity. Floodwater carries dissolved salts, sewage and road runoff, making it a highly conductive electrolyte, often comparable to or better than soil. Hazard also does not require a large absolute current, only a sufficient voltage gradient across a person's stride (step potential) or contact points (touch potential), as defined in IEEE Std 80. That standard uses a reference human body resistance of 1,000 ohms to set tolerable voltage limits.

Antenna placement

An antenna needs to be in free air, so it cannot be fully enclosed. Waterproof antennas exist, but if the antenna is fully submerged, communication fails because radio signals do not propagate effectively through water. To solve this, the team routes a cable from the sealed enclosure (through an IP68 SMA bulkhead feedthrough) to the antenna. The device is mounted on a pole with the enclosure, probes and main electronics at ground level, and the antenna placed at a height above the maximum expected flood level on the same pole. This keeps communication working even during flooding.

Outcome

These concerns exposed genuine gaps in the original design. The resulting improvements, multi-probe localization and the antenna feedthrough, are now presented as deliberate engineering decisions, supported by IEEE Std 80, IEEE Std 81 and IEC 60479-1.
